# Form tracks and category notes

Contents: §kit · §invented · §real · §state apparatus · §physics props · §vehicles ·
§weapons and held items · §decals · §foliage and organic

Common to all: author in world meters, Z up, front −Y, near the origin; build from parts in
bmesh; keep every decision in the build script; primary view in the brief decides where
detail goes.

---

## §kit — modular architecture, ground, barriers

The kit is a **system**, so design the family before any piece.

1. **Module sheet first** (write it into `_family_<name>.py` as constants): module width,
   storey height, wall thickness, plinth/cornice heights, opening sizes (door 0.9 × 2.1),
   reveal depth, sill projection. Pieces are parameterized functions of these constants.
2. **Grid.** Every piece's bbox is a multiple of the minor grid and its pivot sits at the
   bottom-left-back grid corner (`bottom_left_back` = Blender min X, max Y, min Z). The
   validator's `grid.dims` + `pivot.bottom_left_back` enforce it. Protrusions (sills,
   cornices, balconies) either stay inside the module footprint or are separate pieces;
   never let one piece's bbox depend on ornament.
3. **Seam contract.** Adjacent pieces must meet without cracks or z-fighting: identical
   edge profiles at the left/right boundaries (same vertex heights for plinth, string
   course, cornice), coplanar outer face at the same Y, and trims that terminate exactly on
   the module line. Build the boundary profile once in the family module and reuse it.
   Check with `review_context` in the brief (tiles the exported piece; see SKILL.md §7).
4. **Wall thickness** is real (0.3–0.6 m masonry, 0.2 m panel) so window reveals read at
   eye level, and so the back face is flush for interior modules.
5. **Collision:** a hand-built `collider_src` of a few boxes (wall slab, reveal returns,
   no ornament) — `mesh` kind. Never a decimated render mesh (it keeps bumps that snag
   characters).
6. **Openings:** keep piece bounds and pivot on the grid; openings inside may be off-grid
   (a centred 1.25 m window has edges at 0.875 m). Put a `socket_opening` empty at the
   opening's bottom center so inserts (window variants, shutters, bars, state apparatus)
   attach without knowing the numbers.
7. **Depth zone:** every piece in a family has the same bbox depth = wall + ornament zone,
   so siblings stay on-grid. Choose the ornament zone for the deepest projection in the
   family (hoods and cornices project 0.2–0.35 m on real tenements); a shallow zone
   flattens the whole kit.
8. **Mouldings:** `bm_profile_sweep(bm, path, profile, plane_normal=(0,-1,0), strip=(v0, v1),
   u_m=module_width)`; around an opening use a 4-point path (bottom-left, top-left,
   top-right, bottom-right) for mitred corners. Define profiles once in the family module;
   derive the trim-sheet strip heights from the profile lengths × px/m so strips carry no
   stretch.
9. **UVs:** trim sheet + tiling. Large flats: `uv_world_box` at the kit density; mouldings,
   sills, frames: `uv_trim` into the trim sheet's strips. One or two material sets per
   piece; the whole kit ≤ spec cap.
10. **Lightmap UV** is generated by `finish` (category flag); keep pieces reasonably convex
   so it packs well.
11. **Variants** (windows closed/open/boarded/broken/barred/shuttered): one base opening,
   inserts as separate objects built by the family module; export each variant as its own
   asset id unless the spec wants states in one file.
12. **Roofs** are seen from above by an iso/commander camera: give them the same care as
   facades at their density class (chimneys, parapet copings, patches, vents).

## §invented — generic period objects

No single real original exists, so the reference is a **type**: collect 3–6 photos of the
real class of object (period, region) and write the 3–5 features that make it read as that
type into the brief (`style_notes`). Dimensions come from human factors and standards:
seat height 0.45, table 0.75, counter 0.9–1.0, bench length 1.5–2.0, bin 0.9–1.1, kiosk
counter 1.1, door 2.1 × 0.9, handrail 0.9–1.0, step riser 0.15–0.18.

Construction: parts as bmesh boxes/profiles/cylinders → `join` (overlapping parts are fine
for game meshes; interpenetration is invisible and cheaper than booleans) → bevel small
(0.002–0.006 m, 1–2 segments) → weighted normals. Add the "type" features first, surface
detail last.

## §real — recognizable real-type objects

Run **reference-modeling** Phases 0–3 (blueprints, dim gate, silhouette IoU ≥ 0.90, 3D
form). Then:
- Retarget topology to the budget: its SDF/marching-cubes parts are ~1M tris — decimate
  or retopologize to the budget and bake a normal map from the dense original
  (`gam.bake(low, 'NORMAL', size, path, high=dense)`).
- Replace its Cycles procedural materials with baked or library materials.
- For fictional-world projects, **de-brand**: remove badges, logos, real serials and
  trade-dress-specific ornaments; keep proportions and construction. (Irongrip: an unnamed
  country — no real marques, no real insignia, S13.)

## §state apparatus (`state_`)

Spawned at runtime by a commander, so they need placement data:
- `ig_attach` (ground/wall/pole/ceiling), `ig_footprint` [x, z] in meters (engine axes:
  width along glTF X, depth along glTF Z = Blender Y).
- Sockets via `gam.socket(name, loc, forward, up)`: local +Y forward in Blender → glTF −Z,
  the glTF camera convention. Cameras: `socket_view` at the lens. Loudspeakers:
  `socket_speaker` at the horn mouth.
- States in one file (irongrip §0.9 hierarchy): root `<id>` → `<id>_intact` (empty holding
  `<id>_lod0..2`) + `<id>_sabotaged` (one mesh; the client hides it at load) + `<id>_col` +
  sockets. **Recipe, one shared texture:**
  1. Build a *superset* bmesh of every part of both states; `tag_parts` with a group per
     role (base, head, shield, sabotage-only bits, intact-only bits).
  2. `uv_pack` once, `choose_texture_size`, bake base/rough/metal normally; bake AO inside
     `with gam.moved_faces(obj, "part", SABOTAGE_ONLY, (4, 0, 0)):` so state-only parts
     don't darken the others.
  3. `split_parts` into the intact set and the sabotaged set; pose the sabotaged pieces
     with `data.transform(rotate_about(...))` (head yanked on its yoke, shield peeled).
  4. `finish(ctx, intact, lod_group="_intact", extra_children=[sabotaged, socket])`.
  Shared parts (the pole) are duplicated into both sets so each state renders alone. Make
  the sabotaged state readable **from the iso/feed distance**, by pose and silhouette
  first: small torn cables don't read at 20 m. Keep a lit interior behind any smashed
  glass, or it renders as a black hole.
- Pivot: bottom center of the **base/footing**, not of the bbox (an overhanging arm
  shifts the bbox center). Use `pivot: custom` + `pivot_point` on the base center. The
  validator then checks root at origin and ground contact. Wall-mounted: center of the
  mount plate, `"on_ground": false` in the brief.
- Barriers/checkpoints tile on the kit grid — treat them like §kit for seams.

## §physics props (`phys_`)

- Pivot = center of mass; the mesh rests on the ground at export (root sits at COM height).
  `finish` does this; the validator recomputes the volume centroid and checks it.
- `ig_mass` realistic (the validator prints a solid-volume mass for sanity; hollow things
  are lighter). `ig_material` from the enum.
- Colliders: `box` for boxy things, `convex` (≤ 64 verts) otherwise; compound shapes as
  several `_col` children only if the spec allows.
- **Breakables**: `_broken` child with pieces, each piece with its own `_col`.
  Built objects (crates, furniture, pallets) → **part-based**: `tag_parts` before `join`,
  `split_parts` after texturing, `make_broken`. Solids (concrete block, glass pane, plaster
  panel, brick) → `voronoi_fracture(obj, n, inner_mat=...)` with an interior material;
  6–12 pieces.
- Doors/gates: `ig_hinge` point and `ig_hinge_axis` in **glTF space** (`gam.to_gltf`);
  pivot at the hinge line is often simpler for the engine — follow the spec.

## §vehicles

- Budget class `vehicle` (brief: `"budget_class": "vehicle"`). Static blockers can be one
  mesh; anything that animates (doors, wheels, water cannon) gets named child nodes with
  their own pivots at the rotation axis.
- Spend triangles on the silhouette (roofline, wheel arches, glasshouse) at eye level and
  from the iso camera; interiors are a dark tinted-glass suggestion unless the player
  enters.
- Glass: separate mesh, `BLEND`, material name contains `glass`.
- Wheels: 16–24 sided cylinders; LOD2 8–12.

## §weapons and held items (`wpn_`)

- Pivot = grip point (`pivot: custom`, `pivot_point` at the grip where the hand closes),
  barrel/forward along glTF +Z = Blender −Y (front faces −Y like everything else).
- Attachment sockets (`socket_muzzle`, `socket_mag`, `socket_eject`) as empties.
- Detail density is high (seen at 0.5 m in first person): `density_class: hero` or per
  spec, normal map baked from a high poly is normal here.
- Mechanical parts that animate (slide, cylinder, bolt, magazine) as separate child nodes
  with pivots on their motion axes.

## §decals (`dec_`)

Orientation:
- **Wall decals** are a quad facing −Y (glTF +Z), bottom edge on the pivot, 4–5 mm proud.
- **Ground decals** lie flat facing +Z (glTF +Y), centred on the pivot, 5 mm up.
- `finish` re-centres the pivot on the quad and so loses the standoff. Use `pivot: custom`
  with `pivot_point` on the surface plane, or re-apply the offset after `finish`.
- Atlases are shared across many decal glbs. Record regions in the material library so the
  validator checks UVs, and tell the engine to load each atlas once.
- Tiny builds: `"light_build": true`.

Quads (or light cards) with an atlas region; `BLEND` alpha, material name with `decal`;
no collider; bottom-center pivot on the surface plane, facing −Y (so it faces +Z in glTF).
Base color and alpha from a painted/baked PNG; keep the palette rules (chalk only for rebel
marks, etc.).

## §foliage and organic

Poor fit for hard-surface construction. Prefer CC0/bought source (log it in LICENSES.md)
or Blender geometry-nodes scatter, then run the same finish/validate. Alpha `MASK`,
material name containing `foliage`/`leaf`/`hedge`/`weed`. Cards must face the camera
plausibly from both first-person and iso views.
